I think starting from a clean slate is the better choice.

Your previous PRD mixed together many ideas (hacker theme, parallel universe, moon world, personal website, portfolio elements, etc.). For a cinematic experience built around one licensed song, a much stronger design is one with one clear artistic vision.

For this project, I would not build a normal "3D website." I would build what I'd call a Real-Time Interactive Music Film.


---

New Vision

Project Codename

> LUNA // BEYOND REACH



A cinematic WebGL experience.

Not a portfolio.

Not a lyric page.

Not a music player.

Not a game.

It feels like walking inside the emotion of the song.

The lyrics don't appear as subtitles.

Instead...

the world itself sings.

Story

A traveller walks through six impossible worlds.

Nobody speaks.

Nobody appears.

The world slowly changes according to the song.

---

Hidden Events

The best experiences are discovered.

Examples:

Stay still for 20 seconds and a shooting star appears.

Watch the moon long enough and a faint halo forms.

Visit World 3 after World 5 and the water briefly reflects the galaxy.

A rare bird crosses the moon only once per session.


These moments create a sense of discovery without distracting from the music.

AI Integration

Since you're using Google AI Studio, I recommend not pasting the full licensed lyrics into the PRD.

Instead:

Keep the PRD generic and architecture-focused.

Store the licensed lyrics in a separate local JSON or data file that only your application reads.

Let the timeline engine synchronize those lyrics with visual events at runtime.


This keeps the architecture reusable while keeping the licensed content separate from your design documentation.

I would also give the project a stronger identity than "website." The goal should be to create something that feels closer to an interactive short film than a conventional web page—where the browser simply becomes the medium for a cinematic experience.
