# Moon Beyond Reach — Product Requirements Document

**Status:** Draft v1 · **Date:** July 24, 2026 · **Type:** Personal cinematic web experience (single-page, WebGL/R3F)

> A one-line brief for anyone new to this doc: *a six-act, music-driven 3D experience about a love that was never spoken, built to the emotional and craft standard of a short film — not a landing page.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Moon Beyond Reach is a solo-authored, cinematic web experience about unspoken, one-sided love, scored to the licensed Odia track *"Dura Akasare Janha Tie."* The visitor moves through six short acts — night sky, an unreachable moon, still water, a glowing forest, a galaxy, and dawn — built in React Three Fiber with a fully custom shader and audio-reactive layer. There is no photo, no name, no biography: the feeling is rendered entirely through light, water, distance, and sound.

This document reorganizes and tightens the brief you provided. It keeps every idea that's actually achievable at high quality in a browser, cuts or defers the handful that aren't (see §4), and adds a small number of original mechanisms — a hard-coded "unreachable" camera constraint, a scripted silence budget, an entry ritual, an accessible fallback route — that turn the brief's own stated principles into things the code actually enforces, not just intentions.

---

## 2. Vision & Principles

**Core idea:** distance is the whole story. The moon is always visible, never closer. Every system in the piece should reinforce that one idea rather than compete with it for attention.

**Goals**
- A first-viewing experience that lands emotionally without any text explaining what it's "about."
- Craft that holds up on a mid-range phone, not just the developer's desktop.
- A piece that rewards stillness — most of the best moments should be things the visitor notices only if they stop moving.

**Non-goals**
- Not a personal-memory site: no photos, names, dates, or biographical detail. The imagery stays symbolic (moon, water, light), per your brief.
- Not a marketing or portfolio landing page: no hero copy selling anything, no CTA, no lead capture.
- Not literally Pixar/Unreal-fidelity — that's an offline-rendering bar. §4 translates the *feeling* of that quality bar into what a browser can actually sustain at 60fps.

**Guiding principle (from your own closing note, made literal):** *long stretches of calm, interrupted by a few carefully timed moments.* This isn't just a mood — §7 turns it into a scheduling rule the timeline engine actually enforces.

## 4. Reality Check: Scope Calibration

Read this table as "what to actually build" for each ambitious line in the brief. Nothing here lowers the ambition — it's the difference between promising a feature and shipping one.

| As briefed | Why it's not literally buildable for a shipped browser experience | What we build instead |
|---|---|---|
| 8K procedural moon, real craters, orbiting debris, dynamic eclipse | Film-VFX-grade fidelity; 8K textures alone blow mobile memory budgets | 2–4K normal/height maps (public-domain lunar imagery, e.g. NASA source data) + parallax-occlusion mapping for close detail, LOD swap at distance. Eclipse/debris → stretch list |
| Two full galaxies, moving nebula, ultra-real stars | Simulating multiple volumetric galaxies in real time is well beyond browser GPU budgets | One hero galaxy dome (shader starfield + nebula noise) as the real backdrop; a second, distant galaxy as a static, well-lit background card for depth |
| Infinite ocean, real waves, foam | True fluid simulation is offline-render territory | Large tiled plane, Gerstner-wave vertex shader, flow-mapped foam, fog/fresnel horizon fade — this is what actually sells "infinite" in real time |
| Hyper-real per-raindrop physics (drop hits water/leaf/glass/lens/reflection individually) | Per-droplet collision against arbitrary geometry isn't real-time-web feasible | Instanced rain-streak particles + a shared ripple-decal system on the water shader + a camera-lens overlay shader for the "hit the lens" look |
| 120fps guaranteed on all mobile devices | Most phones can't sustain 120fps at this effect budget — promising it breaks the experience for the majority of real visitors | Adaptive quality tiers (§13): 60fps target on capable hardware, dynamic resolution + effect down-tiering to hold 30–60fps everywhere else |
| Fully screen-reader-compatible 3D scene | An abstract WebGL scene has no meaningful DOM/ARIA semantics to expose | A parallel, opt-in text-and-audio route (§16) that carries the same six-act arc for screen-reader and no-WebGL visitors |
| Real-time global illumination | Not practical in a shippable web build | Baked/HDRI image-based lighting + a small number of real-time lights on the interactive elements only |
| Floating Temple / Crystal Cave / extra "parallel universe" zones | Each is effectively its own scene-build; adds weeks per zone | Deferred to the Phase 5 stretch list (§20) — the six core acts are the real story |

## 5. Licensing & Content Rights

Standard due diligence for any project built around a licensed track — not a judgment about what you have, just what to have confirmed before this goes anywhere public.

Two different rights are usually bundled under "I have a license," and they're not the same thing:

1. **Right to listen / personal use** — a streaming subscription or a purchased personal-use file. This is almost certainly what's already covered.
2. **Right to publicly reproduce, distribute, and perform** the recording *and* the lyric text as part of a published website — a separate sync / public-performance / lyric-reproduction clearance, typically through the music publisher and the label.

**Default assumption used throughout this PRD:** treat the build as private/unlisted until (2) is confirmed. Practically:
- The actual audio file and the actual lyric/translation text live only in a local, `.gitignore`d asset folder — never committed to a shared repo, never pasted into project docs, never bundled into a build anyone but you can reach.
- If you later decide to submit this somewhere public (Awwwards, a portfolio, a public URL), that's the moment to confirm the second right — not before.

## 6. Experience Architecture — Six Acts

One continuous scene graph, no page reloads, no loading screens between acts (only the initial Act 0 load is "loading," and it's disguised — see below).

**Act 0 — Threshold.** Complete darkness and silence. A heartbeat. Tiny particles resolve out of nothing. This *is* the loading screen — there is no percentage, no spinner, no "67%" anywhere. The universe assembling itself is the only loading indicator. Ends on a single, deliberate tap: the **Entry Ritual** (§8, Decision 3).

**Act I — Overture.** Night sky opens: stars, Milky Way band, aurora ribbons, fireflies. The moon is visible from the first frame, small and distant. Establishes scale.

**Act II — The Moon.** The emotional core. Tap → ripple + brief brightening; the moon never gets physically closer, no matter what the visitor does (§8, Decision 1). This act has no time limit — it's designed to reward a visitor who just watches.

**Act III — Mirror Water.** A still, reflective lake whose horizon fog and fresnel falloff read as much larger than it is (see §4's ocean substitute). Rain begins here, tied to the audio's dynamics. Moon reflection ripples on contact.

**Act IV — Dream Grove.** Bioluminescent forest, wandering fireflies, falling petals, gentle wind. The most "alive" act — nothing repeats identically within one sitting (seeded per-session noise offsets, not literal infinite uniqueness).

**Act V — Galaxy Drift.** A slow camera flight along a constrained path through the hero galaxy dome. Constellations can be formed by visitor interaction (double-tap), each only once per session.

**Act VI — Dawn.** Weather resolves: rain stops, clouds part, aurora fades, sky lightens. No text, no button, no ask (§8, Decision 4). A few seconds of quiet sunrise, then fade to black.

Emotional arc across all six acts: **longing → acceptance → peace.** The piece should not end on unresolved sadness — dawn is the intended resting note.

## 7. Signature Design Decisions

Original mechanisms layered onto your brief — the "something I can't imagine" ask, addressed as concrete systems rather than more visual effects stacked on top of each other.

1. **The Unreachable Constant.** The minimum camera-to-moon distance is a hard-coded floor in the camera rig itself, not a suggestion honored by content design. Tapping the moon can brighten it, ripple it, never shrink the gap. This is the piece's one signature element — the thesis, encoded as a constraint the code cannot violate.
2. **Silence Budget.** The timeline schema (§10) treats silence as a first-class entry with its own minimum duration, interleaved with lyric cues — restraint enforced by data, not by discipline or memory.
3. **Entry Ritual.** A single explicit tap starts both the audio context and the experience. This isn't just a workaround for browser autoplay policy (which blocks unmuted audio without a user gesture) — it gives the piece a threshold to cross instead of an experience that just starts.
4. **No-Ask Ending.** The Act VI close has zero UI chrome — no email capture, no share button, no "restart" competing for attention. If a replay affordance exists at all, it's one quiet icon that fades in only after several seconds of stillness.
5. **Accessible Poem Fallback.** A parallel, opt-in text-and-audio route (§16) carries the same six beats in prose for reduced-motion, no-WebGL, and screen-reader visitors — nobody is locked out of the *feeling*, only the spectacle layer is optional.

## 8. Interaction Model

| Input | Primary effect | Reduced-motion alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Tap on scene | Ripple burst | Soft opacity pulse |
| Tap on moon | Ripple + brief brightness rise (distance unchanged) | Brightness rise only |
| Long-press | Aurora intensifies | Aurora opacity cross-fades in |
| Double-tap | One constellation forms (once each, per session) | Static constellation fade-in |
| Drag / swipe | Camera orbit **clamped to a narrow arc** — not free-fly | Disabled; replaced by quiet Prev/Next act controls |
| Pinch | Camera dolly within a clamped range | Disabled |
| Device tilt | Subtle parallax shift | Disabled entirely |

The clamped-arc camera (vs. a fully free one) is a deliberate correction: unclamped drag-to-orbit breaks the composed, "cinematic, never robotic" framing the brief itself asks for, and free camera movement tied to device tilt is a known motion-sickness trigger for some visitors.

## 9. Audio-Reactive System

**Architecture:** Howler.js handles playback/decoding; a raw Web Audio `AnalyserNode` (via `Howler.ctx`) does FFT analysis. One `useFrame`-driven hook computes smoothed band values and writes them to a small reactive store — every visual system reads from that single source, none run their own analyser (both a performance requirement and the only way to keep everything visually in sync).

| Band | Smoothing | Drives |
|---|---|---|
| Bass (20–250 Hz) | Fast attack, slow release | Water ripple amplitude, moon emissive pulse, capped camera micro-shake |
| Mid (250 Hz–2 kHz) | Medium | Fog/cloud drift speed, firefly swarm density, tree sway |
| Treble (2–8 kHz) | Slow, heavily damped | Constellation sparkle, bloom intensity, aurora intensity |

**Safety note:** raw FFT values are never bound directly to brightness or bloom — always through damping. Flash rate stays under ~3 changes/second and luminance deltas are capped, consistent with general flash-safety guidance for photosensitive visitors. A "calm mode" toggle removes reactive strobing entirely while keeping ambient motion.

## 10. Living Lyric Engine

Never rendered as a static subtitle block. Each line is data — a timestamp range, an id, and a reference to a registered visual "cue" component (constellation-form, lake-reflection-reveal, mist-condense, firefly-gather, petal-assemble, rain-ripple-reveal, cloud-part-reveal, shooting-star-trail). Each cue fires once.

```jsonc
// assets/licensed/lyrics.json — NOT committed to any shared/public repo.
// "text" is intentionally left for you to fill in locally with your own
// licensed line/translation — it is not included anywhere in this document.
{
  "trackId": "your-track-slug",
  "silenceBudgetSeconds": 2.5, // minimum gap enforced between any two cues
  "entries": [
    { "type": "silence", "start": 0.0, "end": 4.0 },
    { "type": "line", "id": "l1", "start": 4.0, "end": 8.2, "text": "", "cue": "constellation-form" },
    { "type": "silence", "start": 8.2, "end": 10.7 },
    { "type": "line", "id": "l2", "start": 10.7, "end": 15.0, "text": "", "cue": "lake-reflection-reveal" }
  ]
}
```

The engine validates at load time that every `line` entry is preceded by a `silence` entry of at least `silenceBudgetSeconds` — this is what makes §7's Silence Budget an enforced rule rather than a hope.

## 11. Visual Design System

**Palette** — six named tokens, all with a job (no decorative extras):

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `void` | `#05060A` | primary background — the silence |
| `midnight` | `#10172B` | depth layers, fog, secondary surfaces |
| `moon` | `#DDD9E0` | primary light color, sparse text |
| `aurora` | `#5FE0D8` | *reactive* accent only — ripples, taps, treble sparkle |
| `yearning` | `#8E7FCB` | *ambient* accent only — aurora bands, mist |
| `ember` | `#DCB273` | rare warm accent — Act VI dawn only, under 5% of total runtime |

This deliberately avoids the two most common "AI-generated" defaults: a warm-cream-and-terracotta look, and a near-black background paired with one single bright accent. Here, two cool accents are functionally separated (reactive vs. ambient) and the one warm tone is rationed to a single act — the palette does narrative work instead of just decorating.

**Typography — two voices in tension, not blended**, echoing the brief's own "hacker/developer, sad/emotional" duality:
- *Emotional voice* — a high-contrast, slightly irregular serif (e.g. Fraunces at a large optical size, italic-leaning), used **only** for the opening/closing title card. Nowhere else.
- *System voice* — a monospace face (e.g. Space Mono), small, letterspaced, used for **all** UI chrome — captions, credits, the settings affordance — coded visually like a subtitle/terminal track, never like marketing copy.
- The **accessible fallback route** (§16) uses neither — long-form reading needs a plain, highly legible system-UI sans stack. Personality is rationed to the spectacle layer; the accessibility layer optimizes for legibility only.

Text appears rarely, on purpose. When it does, it should feel inevitable, not decorative.

## 12. Visual Effects & Quality Tiers

| Tier | Target hardware | Post-processing | Particle budget | Reflections/shadows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cinematic | High-end desktop/laptop GPU | Bloom, depth of field, screen-space reflection (water only), film grain, vignette, subtle chromatic aberration | Full (~40k instanced) | Real-time soft shadows on hero objects |
| Standard | Mid desktop, high-end mobile | Bloom, cheap bokeh DoF, vignette | ~50% | Baked cubemap reflection, contact shadows |
| Efficiency | Low-end mobile / battery saver / reduced-motion | Bloom only | ~20%, DPR capped at 1 | Ambient only, no shadows |

Tier is auto-detected from a lightweight capability probe (GPU renderer string heuristic + `navigator.deviceMemory` + `prefers-reduced-motion`), with a manual override in the settings affordance.

Stack notes: post-processing via `@react-three/postprocessing`; real-time GI is out of scope (§4) — ambient lighting comes from baked HDRI image-based lighting plus a handful of real lights on interactive elements.

## 15. Performance Budgets

| Tier | FPS target | Max DPR | Total particles | Texture budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cinematic | 60 (120 opportunistic) | 2 | ~40k | ~256MB |
| Standard | 60 | 1.5 | ~15k | ~128MB |
| Efficiency | 30–45 adaptive | 1 | ~5k | ~64MB |

Techniques: instancing for particles/fireflies/petals, frustum culling, KTX2/Basis texture compression, Draco mesh compression, per-act code-splitting (only the active and next act's assets stay resident), `<Suspense>`-based streaming, capped `devicePixelRatio` on mobile, no full real-time shadow maps outside the Cinematic tier.

## 16. Accessibility Plan

- `prefers-reduced-motion` swaps parallax/free camera motion for simple cross-fades and disables tilt-parallax and reactive strobing by default.
- Full keyboard navigation for act progression, replay, and mute — no interaction is mouse/touch-only.
- Every lyric cue has a visible caption option, which doubles as support for hard-of-hearing visitors.
- Audio never autoplays unmuted; a persistent, reachable mute/volume control exists from the first frame after the Entry Ritual.
- Flash-rate and luminance-delta caps on all audio-reactive brightness/bloom changes (§9).
- **Accessible Poem Fallback**: a parallel route rendering the same six-act arc as short prose beats, synced captions, and the audio track, for screen-reader, no-WebGL, and reduced-motion-by-preference visitors. This is the mechanism that answers §4's "screen-reader-compatible 3D scene" ask honestly.

## 22. Success Criteria

- Qualitative: lands emotionally on a first, unprompted viewing.
- Technical: FPS targets held per tier under profiling; sub-3-second time-to-interactive on a mid-range phone over 4G.
- Craft: no two animation loops feel identical within a single sitting (seeded variation, not literal infinite uniqueness).
